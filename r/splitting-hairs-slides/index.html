Splitting Hair(s) Slides

DNA Deep Dive, Part 1 of 2

Every biological item the FBI took off Flight 305 and what became of it: the eight Raleigh cigarette butts destroyed in Las Vegas, the Seat 18-E headrest hair slide last returned to Las Vegas in 1984, and the clip-on tie that gave up a 9-locus DNA mixture in 2002. The report follows how the Lab's reading of that mixture changed through 2011, every suspect compared against it, and what sits in the 58 pages of unreleased bench notes. Part 2, 500 Down, 556 to Go, covers the 2004 subfile audit that picked the first suspects.

Key findings

  • The 1971 items: butts destroyed, hair slide lost. The Raleigh butts were destroyed in Las Vegas; Cooper's hair slide was last sent back there in 1984, and the slide Los Angeles shipped to Seattle in 2002 may not be his.
  • The tie: a 3+ person mixture, exclusion-only. Typed with Profiler Plus in 2002 and searched in CODIS with no hits, downgraded to exclusion-only in 2006, and read as one major and two minor contributors by 2010–2011.
  • 13 suspects: 3 excluded outright. Langseth, the South Lake Tahoe suspect and Gossett were excluded from the full mixture, L.D. Cooper as the major contributor; most of the rest were never tested or their reports are unreleased.
