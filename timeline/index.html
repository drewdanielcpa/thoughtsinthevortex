    Reading the wheel. Marks run in time order, colored by who acted. Taller marks with a gold tip are turning points; the six headline moments also get a dotted gold guide line and a Jump to button. Spacing is half clock time and half entry count, so busy stretches like the jump window get room; the hour ticks show where the clock actually falls. Times marked ≈ are estimates from the documents' wording.

    Search and filters. Turning on a filter or typing a search zooms the wheel out to the whole day and gives the matching marks the room, squeezing the rest between them; clear it to return to the normal view. The search box looks in the timeline entries and in the machine-read text of the FBI pages they cite (each page is credited to one entry, so a page cited many times shows up once). A match found only on a page opens that page and shows the matching line. Abbreviated teletype words ("OSCLLTNS") and OCR misreads can hide a match, so try a shorter word or another spelling.

    Sources. FBI Vault, D.B. Cooper files. Most pages are from the Seattle summary report (Part 110): NWA ground teletype pp. 89–108, NWA handwritten logs pp. 111–134, Minneapolis Flight Ops log pp. 137–143, FAA tape transcripts pp. 146–227, plus crew, agent and police interviews. The NWA engineering analysis is Part 64 p. 299. Log authors' names are redacted in the FBI copies.
